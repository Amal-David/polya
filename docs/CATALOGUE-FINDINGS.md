# Catalogue findings

The historical [source catalogue](https://github.com/floflo777/open-crypto-puzzles/blob/7f691a58c2c6ac60b0d7fd369376f91b48f27cb3/puzzles.json)
is useful for identifying recurring needs, not as a guarantee that the current
library handles any listed case. See [holistic coverage](HOLISTIC-COVERAGE.md)
for snapshot counts and attribution.

Recurring abstractions worth extracting from case-specific scripts include exact
byte normalization, uncertain text/glyph alternatives, grid routes, candidate
products, local dependencies, derivation compatibility, and independent acceptance
checks. Physical evidence, unavailable publications, and cultural references
need explicit missing-information states rather than unbounded computation.

An upstream oracle label is a claim to inspect. Reproduce its positive vectors,
check that acceptance tests the intended final target, and distinguish format
signals from full success. Passing a parser, checksum, or padding test does not
by itself solve a puzzle. Source versions and retractions must remain visible.

Catalogue inclusion, available source code, successful self-tests, real-stage
replay and end-to-end success are separate support levels. None should silently
stand in for the others. Original puzzle assets and their licensing remain with
their respective owners.
